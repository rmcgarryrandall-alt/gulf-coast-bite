Where to start

Dauphin Island Bridge

Fish the down-current side of the pilings and the channel edges. Sheepshead hold tight to the concrete while trout stage off the shadow line.

West End

Walk the bars and cuts on a falling tide. Pompano and reds ride the troughs when the surf turns green.

Aloe Bay

On a hard wind the bay side stays fishable. Pitch shrimp around dock shade and the little drains.

What's biting through the year

Winter (Dec–Feb)

Sheepshead own the bridge, black drum eat in the channels, and redfish school on the flats behind the island for the calm-day sight fishermen.

Spring (Mar–May)

Pompano and Spanish work the west end surf, trout wake up on the grass behind the island, and the bridge starts its all-year redfish shift.

Summer (Jun–Aug)

Early trips beat the heat: reds and trout at daylight, white trout stacked in the channels, snapper on the hard structure, and tarpon rolling off the beach on the calmest mornings.

Fall (Sep–Nov)

The island's best stretch: bull reds at the bridge and Sand Island, the flounder run through every cut (closed in November), and trout ganging in the bay.

Common questions

What can I catch from the Dauphin Island bridge?

Sheepshead tight to the pilings in winter, redfish on the current seams all year, flounder on the bottom during the fall run, and trout staging off the shadow lines at night.

Is the west end of Dauphin Island good for fishing?

Yes — the west end's bars and cuts fish like a wilder Gulf Shores: pompano and whiting in the troughs in spring, reds in the cuts on falling tides, and far fewer footprints.

What lives behind Dauphin Island?

The grass flats and bays behind the island hold trout, redfish, and flounder most of the year, and they stay fishable when the Gulf side is blown out. Current is the key — fish the moving water.
