Fishing charters & guides.

A good inshore guide compresses years of local learning into one morning. This page covers how to pick one on this coast, what to ask before you book, and — for captains — how to get listed in front of anglers who are already checking conditions here every morning.

How to pick a guide here

Match the guide to the water, not the marina. A Perdido Pass jetty specialist, a Mobile Bay delta guide, and a Little Lagoon skiff captain fish three different games. Decide what you want — a cooler of trout, a shot at a bull red, a kid's first fish, sight-fishing — and say so plainly; the honest captains will tell you if the calendar fits. Book mornings over afternoons in warm months, watch the bite score for your trip week, and let the captain move the date if a front lines up wrong. A guide who suggests rescheduling is a guide worth keeping.

Questions worth asking

What will we actually catch?

Ask what's been biting this month, not what the website promises. Cross-check the answer against the site's season watch.

What's included?

License coverage, bait, tackle, ice, and fish cleaning vary. Ask, and ask about the weather-cancellation policy in the same breath.

Your style or theirs?

If you want to throw artificials and the boat is a live-bait operation (or vice versa), say so before the deposit, not at the dock.

The directory

A directory listing includes your waters, target species, season calendar, contact, and a link, placed on this page and on your waters' condition pages.
