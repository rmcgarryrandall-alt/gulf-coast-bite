Species guide

Redfish, from Pensacola to Dauphin Island

The redfish is the most forgiving fish on this coast: it eats year-round, tolerates dirty water and wind, and lives everywhere from the surf to the back of the bayous. If the bite score is low and you're going anyway, you're probably redfishing.

Seasons

Redfish never really leave. Spring and summer spread slot fish across grass flats, oyster edges, and dock lines, feeding hardest on the first moving water of the morning. The event of the year is the fall run: from September into November, schools of oversize bull reds stage around Pensacola Pass, Perdido Pass, and the mouth of Mobile Bay to spawn, and Dixey Bar off Fort Morgan becomes one of the most famous bull red spots in the country. Winter pushes slot fish into deep holes, canals, and dark-bottom flats that warm in the afternoon sun, where they school tight and sight-fish beautifully on clear days.

Tides and water

A redfish trip is a tide trip. Moving water pushes bait across points, through drains, and along seams, and the reds set up down-current to eat. The specific stage matters less than movement itself: the last of the rise and the first of the fall are dependable everywhere on this coast. On big fall tides, look for the seam where pass current meets slack water. On weak neap tides, find the pinch points where whatever current exists gets concentrated: creek mouths, cuts between islands, bridge lanes.

Baits and tackle

Live shrimp under a popping cork catches redfish every day of the year here. A gold spoon is the classic search bait and still the best way to cover a grass flat. Cut mullet or ladyfish soaked on bottom picks out the bigger fish, and a paddletail on a ¼ oz jighead does everything at once. For slot fish, 2500–3000 size spinning gear with 15–20 lb braid and a 20 lb fluorocarbon leader is plenty. For the fall bull run, step up to 4000–5000 size reels, 30–40 lb leader, and enough rod to end the fight quickly — a long fight in warm water is hard on a fish you have to release.

Where to start

Around Pensacola, work the bridge shadow lines and pass seams. At Perdido Key, the pass on moving water and the Old River docks are the everyday game. Orange Beach fishes the same pattern at Alabama Point and the Ono Island canals. Gulf Shores anglers find them in Little Lagoon and along the beach at first light. On Mobile Bay, Dixey Bar is the bull red spot, with slot fish along the causeway rips and shell banks. At Dauphin Island, the bridge, the west end cuts, and the bay side all hold reds whenever the water moves.

Rules in one breath

Florida runs an 18–27" slot with one fish per person and a vessel limit; Alabama runs a 16–26" slot with three per person, one allowed over the slot. Bull reds at the passes are almost all oversize spawners — handle them fast, keep them wet, and get them back.

Common questions

What is the best time of year to catch redfish on the Gulf Coast?

September through November is the peak, when schools of bull redfish stage around Pensacola Pass, Perdido Pass, and the mouth of Mobile Bay. Slot redfish bite reliably all twelve months, which makes them the coast's best bad-day target.

What is the best bait for redfish near Pensacola and Orange Beach?

Live shrimp is the everyday answer, fished under a popping cork or free-lined near structure. A gold spoon covers water on the flats, and cut mullet on bottom selects for bigger fish around the passes.

Where do bull redfish run in Alabama and the Florida Panhandle?

The famous fall spots are Dixey Bar at the mouth of Mobile Bay, Perdido Pass at Orange Beach, and Pensacola Pass. Fish the current seams on a moving tide with heavy jigs, cut bait, or big paddletails.

Can you keep bull redfish?

Generally no. Florida's slot is 18–27 inches and Alabama's is 16–26 with one oversize fish allowed per angler; most pass-run bulls are well above both slots and should be released quickly.
