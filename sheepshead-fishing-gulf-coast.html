Sheepshead, the convict on the pilings

When winter fronts shut down everything flashy, the sheepshead keeps banker's hours on every bridge, jetty, and channel marker on the coast. It bites soft, steals brilliantly, fights dirty around barnacles, and fries as well as anything that swims.

Seasons

Sheepshead live here year-round, but the late-winter spawn is the show: from February into April, the biggest fish of the year gang up on jetties, bridge legs, wrecks, and markers, and a 5-pounder stops being unusual. They bite through cold fronts that ruin everything else, feeding best on the warming days between blows. After the spawn they scatter thin across docks and rocks for the summer, then start drifting back to structure in late fall.

Tides and water

Sheepshead hold tight to vertical structure and feed by picking crustaceans off it, so tide matters less than proximity: the fish are inches from the pilings, and your bait should be too. A light-to-moderate current helps pin them to a predictable side. Clear winter water lets you sight-fish them around shallow rocks on calm days.

Baits and tackle

Fiddler crabs are the bait; bring twice as many as feels reasonable, because the pilings tax you one at a time. Live shrimp, oyster pieces, and barnacles scraped off the very structure you're fishing all work. Rig a small, strong hook — a #1 or 1/0 live-bait hook — with just enough weight to hold vertical against the piling. The bite is a tick you barely feel; set on anything suspicious. 20–30 lb leader survives the barnacles better than pride does.

Where to start

The Pensacola Bay bridge rubble and the Three Mile relics, the Perdido Pass jetties and seawall, the Alabama Point rocks, the Gulf State Park Pier pilings, the channel markers of Mobile Bay, and the Dauphin Island bridge — sheepshead structure is not a secret; the fish just make you earn them anyway.

Rules in one breath

Florida: 12" minimum, eight per person, with a 50-fish vessel limit during the March–April spawn. Alabama: 12" fork-length minimum, eight per person.

Common questions

When is the best sheepshead fishing on the Gulf Coast?

February through April, when spawning fish stack on jetties, bridges, and markers. They also bite well right through winter cold fronts that shut down trout and redfish.

What is the best bait for sheepshead?

Fiddler crabs, fished on a small strong hook tight to the structure. Live shrimp, oyster and barnacle pieces work too. Bring far more bait than you think you need.

How do you detect a sheepshead bite?

Keep the line tight, watch it where it enters the water, and strike at the faintest tick or sideways movement. The joke is you set the hook just before the bite; it's only half a joke.

Do you need a boat for sheepshead?

No. The Perdido Pass seawall, Gulf State Park Pier, Dauphin Island bridge, and Pensacola's bridge fishing piers put shore anglers on exactly the structure the fish use.
